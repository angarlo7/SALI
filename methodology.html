Methodology

How SALI works and what it measures

What is SALI?

The Satoshi Annual Labor Index (SALI) is a measurement tool that expresses your annual income in satoshis—the smallest unit of Bitcoin (1 BTC = 100,000,000 sats). By converting fiat income to satoshis, you can observe how your labor's value changes relative to Bitcoin over time.

SALI is not an investment metric. It's a unit conversion that provides a different perspective on income, one measured against a fixed-supply monetary asset rather than an inflationary fiat currency.

The Formula

SALI (sats/year) = (Annual Salary in USD ÷ BTC Price in USD) × 100,000,000

The calculation is straightforward:

  1. Take your annual salary in your local currency
  2. Convert it to USD (if not already in USD)
  3. Divide by the current Bitcoin price in USD
  4. Multiply by 100,000,000 to express in satoshis

The result tells you how many satoshis your annual labor could theoretically acquire at the given Bitcoin price.

Bitcoin Price Selection

SALI offers three methods for selecting the Bitcoin price used in calculations:

Spot Price

The current market price fetched from CoinGecko's public API. This reflects real-time market conditions but can be volatile. Use this for a snapshot of your current SALI.

Annual Average

Historical annual average prices smooth out daily volatility. The calculator uses the most recent year's average as the baseline. This method is useful for year-over-year comparisons that aren't distorted by short-term price swings.

Manual Entry

Enter any Bitcoin price manually. This is useful for:

  • Historical calculations using specific past prices
  • Scenario analysis ("What if BTC were at $X?")
  • Using prices from other sources or exchanges
  • Offline usage when API calls fail

Projections

The projection feature extends your SALI calculation over multiple years using simple compounding assumptions:

Salary (Year N) = Salary (Year 0) × (1 + Salary Growth Rate)^N

BTC Price (Year N) = BTC Price (Year 0) × (1 + BTC Growth Rate)^N

These are not predictions. They're mechanical extrapolations that show how your SALI would evolve if the growth rates you specify were to hold constant. Real-world salary increases and Bitcoin prices are far more variable.

The Year-over-Year (YoY) change shows the percent difference between Year 0 and Year 1 SALI values, indicating the direction and magnitude of change given your assumptions.

What SALI Is Not

To use SALI responsibly, understand its limitations:

  • Not a price prediction. SALI does not forecast Bitcoin's future price. The projection feature uses your inputs mechanically—it has no predictive power.
  • Not an inflation model. SALI doesn't account for CPI, real purchasing power, or cost of living. It's purely a unit conversion to satoshis.
  • Not financial advice. SALI is an educational tool. It doesn't tell you to buy Bitcoin, hold Bitcoin, or make any financial decision.
  • Not a complete picture. Your financial health depends on many factors beyond how your salary compares to Bitcoin.

Limitations

Bitcoin Volatility

Bitcoin's price can move 10-20% in a single day. Your SALI will fluctuate accordingly. A high SALI during a price dip doesn't mean your labor is worth more—it means Bitcoin is temporarily cheaper.

Relative Benchmark

SALI measures your income against Bitcoin specifically. It doesn't compare to gold, real estate, stocks, or actual purchasing power. Bitcoin is a particular benchmark with its own characteristics.

Assumptions Matter

Projection accuracy depends entirely on your growth rate assumptions. If you assume 50% annual BTC price growth and it grows 10%, your projections will be wrong. Use conservative ranges and understand that projections illustrate sensitivity, not destiny.

Currency Conversion

Non-USD salaries are converted using static placeholder exchange rates. For accurate results with EUR, MXN, or other currencies, consider using the Manual price entry with your own calculations.
